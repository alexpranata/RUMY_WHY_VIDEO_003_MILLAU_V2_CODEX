# Millau Viaduct — verified engineering facts and illustration boundaries

For on-screen facts, prefer these sources and verified values. This is a documentary, so CGI is illustrative only, not archival footage or validated technical simulation.

| Fact | Value and boundaries | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Location | A75 motorway, Tarn Valley near Millau, southern France | Eiffage, ICE |
| Bridge length | 2,460 m / ~1.53 mi | Eiffage, Official |
| Highest overall point | 343 m / ~1,125 ft **to the top of a pylon**, NOT roadway height | Eiffage, ICE |
| Highest concrete pier | P2 approx. 245 m / 804 ft, separate from maximum overall height | Official, ICE |
| Piers | 7 main reinforced-concrete piers, hollow at height, with forked upper sections | ICE, Official |
| Foundations | Piers founded on four deep reinforced-concrete shafts into the geology; CGI shafts are schematic unless dimensions verified | ICE |
| How piers grew | Self-climbing formwork, approximately 4 m every 3 days during construction; represent as staged time lapse (NOT instant solidification) | Official |
| Deck | Prefabricated steel sections were brought and assembled/welded in staging areas on BOTH sides of valley | ICE, Official |
| Deck launch | 64 hydraulic translators; 18 launching operations; translations approx. 60 cm per translator cycle; average launching operation ~9m per hour | Official |
| Support during launching | Permanent concrete piers + red steel temporary support towers; one partially stay-supported pylon at the leading edge on EACH of the two launch fronts | Official, Freyssinet |
| Closure | 28 May 2004, northern and southern deck halves met high over Tarn River | Official, Eiffage |
| Pylons | 2 supported launch early; **remaining 5** installed after joining the decks, raised from horizontal on the deck | Official |
| Completed deck mass | 36,000 metric tonnes is TOTAL completed deck mass. **NEVER** claim simultaneously pushed as one single 36,000t slug or lifted by one crane | Official, Eiffage |
| Cable-stay arrangement | Final bridge has 7 pylons, 154 stays | Freyssinet |
| Completion | Dec 2004; inaugurated 14 Dec 2004 | Eiffage |
| Site challenges | Steep valley, weather and strong wind; launch operations restricted for strong winds | Greisch, Official |

## Critical technical corrections

1. Do not depict the deck being raised as one continuous 1.5-mile piece by a crane. Deck sections were assembled on land, then progressively launched from each side.
2. Don't put seven completed pylons on the deck while it is being launched: leading pylons on each launch front first; five later.
3. Don't depict the permanent seven-pylon bridge with red temporary towers; those belong only to construction.
4. Don't show workers standing freely on unsafe high steel surfaces without harnesses, railings or platforms.
5. Four deep drilled foundations per pier can be shown as a **representative schematic**; not claim exact number, depth or diameter beyond sourced details.
6. Concrete curing must not be depicted as magically completed instantaneously; express construction progression as stylized time lapse with distinct steps.
7. Avoid showing a floating unsupported steel cantilever or deck momentarily resting on nothing. Use red supports and stay assistance appropriately.
8. Use pylon mast-to-foundation measure only for 343m overall: overlay should explicitly say MAX STRUCTURAL HEIGHT, not ROAD HEIGHT.
9. Old audio says 36,000 tonnes total steel deck. It is a total final weight, not a single dynamic force, and visuals must clarify this.
10. Reference example from the Mississippi is **stylistic** only. Do not treat its footage or narration as source material to include in final.
