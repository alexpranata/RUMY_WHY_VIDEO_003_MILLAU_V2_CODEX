How do engineers build a bridge taller than the Eiffel Tower... across an enormous valley?

This is the Millau Viaduct in southern France. It stretches one point five three miles across the Tarn Valley, and reaches one thousand one hundred twenty-five feet at its highest point.

But the biggest challenge wasn't the height. It was getting the roadway across.

First, engineers built seven towering concrete piers, anchored into the ground. The tallest rises eight hundred four feet.

Then came the extraordinary part.

Instead of assembling the entire roadway high above the valley, workers built long sections of steel deck on the ground, at both ends.

Using sixty-four hydraulic launching devices, they pushed the deck sections forward, little by little.

Temporary steel towers and supporting cables helped keep the structure stable as it crossed the gaps.

After eighteen launching operations, the two sides finally met above the Tarn River in May 2004.

Engineers finished the pylons and stay cables, and the bridge opened that December.

Thirty-six thousand metric tons of steel deck... assembled on land, then pushed across the sky.
